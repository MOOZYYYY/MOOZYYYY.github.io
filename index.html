Customer Segmentation: Who should a retailer protect, grow, or let go?

A UK online gift retailer sends the same marketing to every customer. I segmented 4,320 customers from 540k+ transactions with RFM analysis and K-means clustering, compared both methods, and turned the results into an action and budget priority for each group.

64%of revenue from the VIP cluster (16% of customers)
74%of revenue from the top 20% of customers
1 in 3customers ordered only once
0.996cluster stability (Adjusted Rand Index)

What I did

  • Cleaned 541,909 transactions, netting out £472k of cancellations instead of deleting them (one customer ordered 80,995 units and cancelled 12 minutes later)
  • Built RFM scores and 10 rule-based customer segments
  • Showed K-means fails on raw skewed data, then log-transformed and chose k = 4 using elbow, silhouette and business usefulness

Key insight

  • The two methods broadly agree, but K-means hid churn risk: 89% of high-value customers who had gone quiet were placed in an "ordinary" cluster
  • Recommendation: use K-means for strategy and RFM rules as churn alerts; focus on protecting VIPs and winning the second order from new customers
